# USER_INTERVIEWS.md

# User Interview Notes

These interviews were conducted to better understand how startups and small teams currently manage AI software spending, how they discover inefficiencies, and what would make an AI spend audit platform valuable in real-world usage.

The conversations were informal 10–15 minute discussions focused on:
- AI tool adoption
- Subscription management
- Cost visibility
- Team workflows
- Optimization pain points

---

# Interview 1

## Name
R.K.

## Role
Frontend Developer & Freelance SaaS Builder

## Company Stage
Solo developer building small SaaS products and client projects

---

# Key Quotes

> “I subscribe to AI tools quickly whenever they improve my workflow, but I rarely cancel old ones.”

> “I honestly don’t know my total monthly AI spending unless I manually check bank statements.”

> “Some tools overlap heavily, but switching feels annoying once the workflow is established.”

> “I’d definitely use a simple audit tool if it gave realistic savings recommendations.”

---

# Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising thing was that even a solo developer with relatively low spending had already accumulated multiple overlapping AI subscriptions without tracking them intentionally.

The user specifically mentioned paying simultaneously for:
- ChatGPT Plus
- Cursor
- Claude
- GitHub Copilot

despite using some tools only occasionally.

---

# What It Changed About My Design

This conversation made me simplify the onboarding process significantly.

Initially, I planned to ask users for detailed usage metrics, but I realized most people do not want to spend time entering complicated data.

As a result:
- The audit form became shorter
- Inputs became more startup-friendly
- The platform focused on fast estimates rather than perfect financial analysis

---

# Interview 2

## Name
S.M.

## Role
Startup Founder

## Company Stage
Seed-stage SaaS startup with 8 employees

---

# Key Quotes

> “AI spending grows quietly because everyone on the team buys tools independently.”

> “The bigger issue isn’t one expensive tool — it’s ten small subscriptions adding up.”

> “We optimize cloud infrastructure aggressively, but nobody really optimizes AI software yet.”

> “I’d want benchmarking data showing whether our spending is normal compared to similar startups.”

---

# Most Surprising Insight

The founder explained that their team had formal tracking for:
- Cloud infrastructure
- Advertising
- Payroll

but almost no visibility into AI subscription costs despite increasing dependence on AI workflows.

This highlighted that AI software spending is currently treated differently from traditional operational expenses.

---

# What It Changed About My Design

This conversation influenced several product ideas:
- Benchmark analytics
- Team-based audit reporting
- Multi-user organization support
- Historical spend tracking

It also reinforced the idea that the product should feel operational and financial rather than purely AI-focused.

---

# Interview 3

## Name
A.J.

## Role
Agency Owner

## Company Stage
Small creative agency with 5 team members

---

# Key Quotes

> “Every designer and developer prefers different AI tools, so subscriptions become chaotic.”

> “The problem isn’t discovering AI tools anymore. The problem is deciding which ones are actually worth paying for.”

> “I don’t need complicated analytics. I just want to know where we’re wasting money.”

> “A short professional report is more useful than a giant dashboard.”

---

# Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising takeaway was that simplicity mattered more than advanced analytics for smaller teams.

The user specifically preferred:
- Fast insights
- Short summaries
- Clear savings estimates

over complex reporting systems.

This contradicted my earlier assumption that users would want highly detailed dashboards immediately.

---

# What It Changed About My Design

After this conversation:
- I reduced unnecessary dashboard complexity
- Prioritized concise AI-generated summaries
- Focused the UI on actionable recommendations
- Simplified the results layout

It also validated the decision to keep the platform lightweight instead of turning it into a large enterprise-style admin dashboard.

---

# Overall Patterns Across Interviews

Several common themes appeared repeatedly:

## 1. AI Spending Is Poorly Tracked

Most users:
- Adopt AI tools quickly
- Rarely centralize spending
- Do not know total monthly AI costs

---

## 2. Subscription Overlap Is Common

Many teams pay for:
- Multiple coding assistants
- Multiple writing tools
- Multiple chat models

without intentional optimization.

---

## 3. Simplicity Is Extremely Important

Users preferred:
- Fast audits
- Clear savings estimates
- Short summaries

instead of:
- Heavy dashboards
- Complex analytics
- Long onboarding flows

---

## 4. AI Tooling Feels Operationally Different

Several users treated AI spending differently from:
- Cloud infrastructure
- Payroll
- Traditional SaaS tools

This creates an opportunity for dedicated AI spend optimization products.

---

# Final Reflection

These interviews changed the direction of the project significantly.

Originally, I planned to build:
- A highly analytical dashboard
- Deep AI-driven recommendations
- Complex reporting workflows

Instead, the conversations pushed the product toward:
- Simplicity
- Speed
- Clear savings visibility
- Lightweight reporting
- Actionable summaries

The interviews also validated that AI spend management is becoming a real operational problem for startups and small teams as AI adoption accelerates.
